# Known issues and open questions

Known limitations, behaviour not yet investigated, oddities to follow up on, and performance worth chasing. Each is either a question for the design, or a fault to look into; once one is settled, the decision goes into [the design](DESIGN.md) or the model's own doc, and it leaves this list.

- **Waiting points:** where an unprotected turner should wait inside a junction, clear of both the traffic behind it and the traffic it gives way to, for any junction geometry. The first conflict with oncoming traffic serves the procedural scenarios: with connectors sized by the swept path, and far-side turns running straight on into the junction before they turn, it is several metres in, so a turner at the head of its lane turns on the first green after it reaches its line, through a gap or as the oncoming traffic stops. How far to run on is a parameter for now. Drivers turning across traffic pull in as far as they can without making the turn too tight, while on a protected turn they turn so as to steer least; a rule from the geometry could replace it. Hook turners' waiting lines come from the road on the near side (see [the design](DESIGN.md), Intersection control).
- **Waiting lines in world space** (**Suggestion**, a potential addition if its merits become worth it): barriers' lines (see [the design](DESIGN.md), Barriers and signals) could hold a turner part way round a curve by where its body actually is, its line where it would meet the traffic it gives way to, and the queue would still order such a vehicle by who it gives way to. In the procedural scenarios a turner waits in much the same place without it; hook turners' waiting lines are where it would pay.
- **Shared lane edges:** neighbouring lanes each store their own vertices, so their ends match only to the grid. Lanes defined as borders offset from one reference line, as OpenDRIVE's lane sections are, would make them match exactly; nothing needs it yet.
- **Gridlock in grids:** flooded with random routes, the 4 by 4 grid of 100 m blocks gridlocks within 15 minutes in every stress run with a car tried every 20 steps, and with one lane each way every 40, but not at half that: typically a turner waiting inside one crossroads for room on a full link, whose queue waits on the next crossroads, whose own queue backs up the opposite link to the turner. Room beyond keeps vehicles from entering with nowhere to go, but not from being trapped as the link fills while they wait for a gap. That is allowed, and real, but it comes quickly: a 16 by 16 grid of 100 m blocks gridlocks within a minute at 10,000 vehicles, where one of 200 m blocks keeps about two in five moving. Whether drivers should do more to avoid it, such as not pulling in to wait for a gap while the way out is nearly full, is open.
- **Junction lockups:** busy signalised crossroads locked up now and then under earlier designs of the queue, each time a car was queued between a waiting turner's two stretches, blocked by it and blocking it. The queue now keeps an order agreeing with every decided pair and every vehicle following another, which holds no cycle, and barriers defer to it (see [the design](DESIGN.md), Queue invariants), and the stress test hasn't locked up. A barrier rule that waits on a vehicle the order doesn't know of can still close a cycle the junction can't see: each the stress test has found was a barrier's rule disagreeing with the junction's, room beyond counting a body alongside at a fork, a gap check judging a car stuck behind one that gives way to it, or the junction not counting a body at a fork as led by it.
- **Turners waiting in each other's way:** on the 60° crossroads, opposite far-side turners wait with each one's body in the other's turn. That no longer locks up, as their pair is decided and the higher goes, but it looks wrong, and breaks the condition the queue's argument rests on, that a turner's waiting line comes before its first conflict with the traffic it gives way to. Compilation could check that no waiting body is in another turner's way, and set waiting lines back, or shape the turns, where one is.
- **Lifts refused for a pair the walk hasn't reached:** ordering lifts a vehicle over the nearest above it first, so a lift isn't refused because of an edge a later lift would reverse; but a lift can still be refused for an edge the rule would reverse once some other pair moved, and the vehicle then takes a term for a macro step, braking harder than it needs to. Treating such edges as part of the same lift would fix it.
- **Going behind a slowing vehicle:** the gap check takes a priority vehicle's speed now, though it may slow for a turn ahead, so it can expect one to clear sooner than it will, and the junction then holds the yielding vehicle back for it.
- **Critical gaps:** from rest at a give-way line onto a 50 km/h road with one lane each way, a car takes lags down to about 7.4 s straight across, 7.6 s turning near-side and 9.3 s turning far-side, merging with the far side's traffic, where the Highway Capacity Manual has about 6.5, 6.2 and 7.1 s, the car with priority braking at most 1.3 m/s² for it: a second or two cautious. The `critical_gaps` example in `network` measures them.
- **Cost of measuring gaps for the lane-change model:** moving the gap checks out of LMRS into the core's measurements slowed a step of the junction and city benchmarks by 5–13%, partly from measuring more gaps and partly from a slightly different traffic state; not yet profiled.
- **Merge capacity:** relaxed headways let a merged lane carry about 2,600 veh/h, above the 2,000–2,400 of real lanes.
- **Replay tests** (future work): the UI's replay format, and its reader in `ui/src/replay/read.rs`, are a hack for tests. Tests should build the minimal situation they check programmatically, as the rest of the suite does, not replay an ad hoc log; the replay round-trip test and the capture-to-test workflow should go once nothing relies on them.
- **Two drivers changing into one spot from either side:** on the busy three-lane ring, two drivers indicating into the middle lane from either side, level with each other, can both judge the gap clear in the same update and change together, one stopping from rest at full braking (9 m/s²) behind the other. Each counts the other's ghost neither as a leader nor, since the follower search passes ghosts over, as a follower, so neither waits. Asking for gaps a step early (see [Lane changes](models/lane-change.md)) made it rare again; the hazard itself is untouched.
- **Cost per vehicle grows with the city:** on kept grids of 200 m blocks at the same density, a step costs about 0.14 µs per vehicle at 16 by 16 (10,000 vehicles), 0.24 µs at 32 by 32 and 0.40 µs at 64 by 64, roughly N^1.4, which caps real time at about 100,000 vehicles. Likely cache misses as the vehicles and occupant lists outgrow the caches, or work scaling with the network, such as removal scanning every lane's list and every junction's queue. Not yet profiled.
- **Room beyond only at lines:** a vehicle checks for room past a junction only at a barrier into it, signal, give-way or stop line (see [Gap acceptance](models/gap-acceptance.md), Room beyond). Uncontrolled intersections have no barriers, and nor do priority roads at a priority intersection, so their traffic enters with no check and can stop in the junction behind a queue, held apart only by the junction backstop.
- **Room beyond counts only its own way in:** the room it checks on the lane out is for itself and the vehicles ahead of it on its own way through to that lane, so vehicles from other approaches bound for the same lane, even committed ones, aren't counted, and two approaches can each see room for one and both enter. It looks only at the first lane out, so a short, empty lane with a queue straight after it counts as room; the scenarios' roads are 100 m or more.
- **Routes ignore delays:** route tables count only each road's time at its speed limits, not signals, give-way lines or queues, so drivers don't avoid a busy crossroads or a slow far-side filter turn. At a busy three-lane signalised crossroads with destinations drawn evenly, a third of the cars from each approach turn across from one lane, more than the filter turn can carry, and its queue grows. Costs from measured travel times, refreshed now and then, as SUMO's rerouting devices do, would spread them out, at the cost of a table rebuilt per destination.
- **Route tables and lane sections:** a lane change counted in a route table must be done before the two lanes it is between run out beside each other, though a change may run on into the next stretch of lanes side by side where they go on together. Roads are compiled as one stretch each, so it is exact there; a road split into short stretches, each shorter than a change, would have no ways through its changes.
- **Hook turns' one place to wait:** a hook turner waits at one point beside the near-side road's stop line, so a second waits behind it, across the mouth of the lane in. With one lane in, that holds up the traffic going straight on until the next cycle, and the approach saturates at about one hook turn a cycle: in the `hook-turns` scenario, by about 200 vehicles an hour per lane in, a third turning right, where ordinary right turns, waiting in the middle, still keep up. Real hook turners queue along the near side, two or three deep, on wider roads with more than one lane in. Waiting points further along the near side, or a connector that drifts across sooner, would give the second room; whether to, and how far, is open.
- **Connector shapes from swept paths:** a connector's width follows the widest vehicle's swept path, so its outline widens on the inside of bends and tapers in and out, and on a bend too tight for its width, as a hook turn's, it narrows on the inside instead, so as not to fold. The shapes look odd, and conflicts between connectors come from these outlines. A better way to size connectors, and to keep sharp turns from folding, is open.
