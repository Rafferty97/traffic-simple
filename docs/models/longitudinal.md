# Longitudinal model: IDM+

How fast each vehicle wants to go and how hard it brakes, behind the module boundary `models::acceleration`. The core asks it for every term of a vehicle's acceleration, so it is a module boundary only, replaced by editing it, never dispatched at runtime. Its per-vehicle state, the acceleration noise, whether a vehicle is finishing a stop, how far short of its next barrier it stops and its own random generator, is its own, in a map only it reads (see [the design](../DESIGN.md), Simulation state and data layout).

IDM+ (Schakel, van Arem and Netten, 2010) computes each vehicle's acceleration every `macro_step` steps. IDM+ splits IDM into a free-road term and an interaction term and takes the minimum instead of the sum, which gives a more realistic capacity and extends naturally to several leaders: the acceleration is the running minimum over the free-road term and one term per constraint, computed as they are found, with no list built.

- **What it reacts to:** the leaders along its path (below), the end of its known path, lower speed limits ahead, its old side while straddling, junction and merge terms (see [the design](../DESIGN.md), Conflicts, merging and junctions, and Merges), synchronisation and the room it keeps while wanting to change lanes (see [Lane changes](lane-change.md)), a ghost it makes room for, a speed cap, and the first barrier on its path it hasn't committed to (see [the design](../DESIGN.md), Barriers and signals).
- **Which leaders:** a vehicle in the lane that this one can't pass, B, blocks the lane's whole width, so nothing behind it on a shared path can reach anything beyond it that B can't. So once the search finds B, it skips the in-lane vehicles beyond it, on B's lane and on the lanes after it, as far as B's path goes along this one's. This is one high-water mark, the first lane of the path that no in-lane leader covers. A vehicle converging onto the lane can't be passed and shields like any other. Ghosts only partly block a lane, and B may pass a vehicle diverging from the lane that this one can't, so neither is skipped, and neither shields anything. Each vehicle found is its own term, so a car whose leader turns off sees the queue beyond. Treiber's Human Driver Model reacts to several leaders too.
- **Horizon:** it looks ahead as far as s*, the desired gap approaching a stopped leader, `s0 + v·T + v² / (2·√(a·b))`, where a stopped leader's term is zero, or 5 m if that is further, so a leader in a queue at rest doesn't flicker in and out of view. A leader followed at its own speed, `s0 + v·T` behind, is always in view. One coming into view can change the acceleration abruptly, much as a driver catching up with stopped traffic brakes firmly at first; SUMO instead looks as far as braking distance, and Treiber's simulator looks at every leader. A vehicle that wants to change lanes looks at least as far as the room it keeps (see [Lane changes](lane-change.md), Room to finish).
- **End of the known path:** a stop line, a stationary virtual leader, unless the vehicle leaves the network there. Paths reach past the horizon, so it only binds where routing doesn't know the next lane.
- **Visiting order:** lane by lane, from the front of each lane's occupants back, each vehicle's search starting just after its own record, on the lane it follows, or, while it straddles, on its old lane. On a loop shorter than its horizon a vehicle can find itself ahead, which is geometrically true. SUMO plans each lane's vehicles from the front back in the same way.
- **Gaps:** a searching vehicle's rear is its record's s, and its front its length ahead, so every gap is a subtraction along the lanes searched.
- **Desired speed:** the lane's speed limit times the vehicle's speed factor, capped at its maximum speed, as SUMO's `speedFactor`; the factor's distribution is the caller's concern (the UI's: normal, mean 1, deviation 0.05, clamped to 0.85–1.15; SUMO's default deviation is 0.1).
- **Above the desired speed:** the free-road term is the Improved IDM's, `−b·(1 − (v0/v)^(aδ/b))`, slowing toward the desired speed at no more than comfortable deceleration, where IDM's would brake as hard as it could well above it.
- **Finishing a stop:** IDM eases a vehicle up to a stationary leader exponentially, so its speed never quite reaches zero, and it creeps on after a leader that edges forward. So each vehicle has a stopping state: it starts once the vehicle is slowing below 3 km/h, and while it lasts the vehicle brakes at least at 0.5 m/s², or harder if another term asks, one more term, so it comes to rest promptly; it ends once the vehicle could accelerate at 0.2 m/s², so a stopped vehicle doesn't creep after a leader that edges forward, and takes a moment to react as a light turns green. Starting and ending at different accelerations, it doesn't flick between stopping and moving off. It leaves a vehicle a few centimetres further back than IDM would.
- **Lower limits ahead:** a lane ahead on its path with a lower limit, at the driver's share of it, is its own term, `(v_lim² − v²) / 2d` for one `d` ahead of its front, once that is harsher than comfortable braking, and capped at twice that, so it slows before the limit rather than once on it, braking at about its comfortable deceleration. Lanes further ahead than it could stop from its speed comfortably can't matter, so the search goes no further. A lane its front is already on, but its rear axle isn't, counts as the free road's term at that lane's limit.
- **Ghosts:** the search reports every ghost it meets and the caller decides what one means. Checking a gap for a lane change, and in `try_insert`, a ghost is a leader, since a vehicle already indicating into the gap goes first. In the longitudinal model, the nearest ghost before any leader is one the vehicle may make room for.
- **Relaxation:** a vehicle that has just appeared in a lane's traffic, or that another has just appeared in front of, keeps a shorter time headway at first and drops back gradually, as in LMRS (Schakel, Knoop and van Arem, 2012). A record's age starts at zero when the vehicle changes lanes into the lane, enters a merge onto it or joins it from beside it, carries over from one lane to the next, and starts settled for an inserted vehicle. A leader term keeps `h(a)` of the follower's time headway, with `a` the younger of the two records' ages and `h` rising from LMRS's `T_min/T_max`, 0.56/1.2, back to 1 exponentially with a time constant of 25 s. The minimum gap stays whole.
- **Acceleration noise:** drivers don't hold a speed exactly. Each has a slowly varying noise added to its free-road term, an Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process with a time constant of 10 s and a per-driver spread (`accel_noise`), from its own seeded generator, so runs repeat, as in Treiber and Kesting's IDM with acceleration noise (2017). Following stays exact, so the noise never brings vehicles closer than IDM would.
- **Friction:** a driver goes at most 30 km/h faster than the traffic ahead in the lane beside it, the average over the next 8 s at that lane's limit, if at least three vehicles are there: the friction effect Jang and Cassidy (2012) measured beside congested lanes.
- **Parameters** come from each vehicle's attributes. `VehicleSpec::passenger_car` gives textbook values: IDM's as calibrated with LMRS, as OpenTrafficSim uses them, a = 1.25 m/s², b = 2.09 m/s², s0 = 3 m, T = 1.2 s, and an acceleration noise of 0.2 m/s².
