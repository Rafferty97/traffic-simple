# Longitudinal model: IDM+

How fast each vehicle wants to go and how hard it brakes, behind the module boundary `models::acceleration`. The core asks it for every term of a vehicle's acceleration, so it is a module boundary only, replaced by editing it, never dispatched at runtime. Its per-vehicle state, the acceleration noise, whether a vehicle is finishing a stop, how far short of its next barrier it stops and its own random generator, is its own, in a map only it reads (see [the design](../DESIGN.md), Simulation state and data layout).

IDM+ (Schakel, van Arem and Netten, 2010) computes each vehicle's acceleration every `macro_step` steps. IDM+ splits IDM into a free-road term and an interaction term and takes the minimum instead of the sum, which gives a more realistic capacity and extends naturally to several leaders: the acceleration is the running minimum over the free-road term and one term per constraint, computed as they are found, with no list built.

- **What it reacts to:** the leaders along its path (below), the end of its known path, lower speed limits ahead, its old side while straddling, junction and merge terms (see [the design](../DESIGN.md), Conflicts, merging and junctions, and Merges), synchronisation and the room it keeps while wanting to change lanes (see [Lane changes](lane-change.md)), a ghost it makes room for, a speed cap, and the first barrier on its path it hasn't committed to (see [the design](../DESIGN.md), Barriers and signals).
- **Which leaders:** a vehicle in the lane that this one can't pass, B, blocks the lane's whole width, so nothing behind it on a shared path can reach anything beyond it that B can't. So once the search finds B, it skips the in-lane vehicles beyond it, on B's lane and on the lanes after it, as far as B's path goes along this one's. This is one high-water mark, the first lane of the path that no in-lane leader covers. A vehicle converging onto the lane can't be passed and shields like any other. Ghosts only partly block a lane, and B may pass a vehicle diverging from the lane that this one can't, so neither is skipped, and neither shields anything. Each vehicle found is its own term, so a car whose leader turns off sees the queue beyond. Treiber's Human Driver Model reacts to several leaders too. A vehicle it passes by the pass check's projection is a term too (see Passing).
- **Passing:** a vehicle the core lets it pass by the room beside it, at least 0.3 m (see [Multi-lane car following](../multilane-car-following.md), Pass check), it still follows as it goes by, as a leader further ahead and faster: the gap to it, floored at nothing once level, lengthened by 50 m, and its speed raised by 10 m/s, for each metre of room to spare. With none to spare it follows the other vehicle as any leader, so nothing jumps as the room crosses where it may pass; with more, the other soon stops mattering, the further back the sooner, and it goes by slower the tighter the room, as drivers do: about 4 m/s beside a stopped car with 0.2 m to spare, and a crawl with 5 cm. Raising the speed does more than lengthening the gap: with a quarter of a metre to spare, the closing speed IDM sees cancels the time headway it would keep, so it passes a car beside it at speed without dropping back. This is the simulator's own; road design ties lateral clearance to speed through shy distances. With under about 3 cm to spare, the gap is still shorter than the minimum gap, so a car beside a stopped one waits rather than creeping by.
- **Horizon:** it looks ahead as far as s*, the desired gap approaching a stopped leader, `s0 + v·T + v² / (2·√(a·b))`, where a stopped leader's term is zero, or 5 m if that is further, so a leader in a queue at rest doesn't flicker in and out of view. A leader followed at its own speed, `s0 + v·T` behind, is always in view. One coming into view can change the acceleration abruptly, much as a driver catching up with stopped traffic brakes firmly at first; SUMO instead looks as far as braking distance, and Treiber's simulator looks at every leader. A vehicle that wants to change lanes looks at least as far as the room it keeps (see [Lane changes](lane-change.md), Room to finish).
- **End of the known path:** a stop line, a stationary virtual leader, unless the vehicle leaves the network there. Paths reach past the horizon, so it only binds where routing doesn't know the next lane.
- **Visiting order:** lane by lane, from the front of each lane's occupants back, each vehicle's search starting just after its own record, on the lane it follows, or, while it straddles, on its old lane. On a loop shorter than its horizon a vehicle can find itself ahead, which is geometrically true. SUMO plans each lane's vehicles from the front back in the same way.
- **Gaps:** a searching vehicle's rear is its record's s, and its front its length ahead, so every gap is a subtraction along the lanes searched.
- **Desired speed:** the driver's, from the lane's speed limit and its speed factor (see [the driver model](driver.md)).
- **Above the desired speed:** the free-road term is the Improved IDM's, `−b·(1 − (v0/v)^(aδ/b))`, slowing toward the desired speed at no more than comfortable deceleration, where IDM's would brake as hard as it could well above it.
- **Finishing a stop:** IDM eases a vehicle up to a stationary leader exponentially, so its speed never quite reaches zero, and it creeps on after a leader that edges forward. So each vehicle has a stopping state: it starts once the vehicle is slowing below 3 km/h, and while it lasts the vehicle brakes at least at 0.05 m/s², or harder if another term asks, one more term, so it comes to rest in a few seconds rather than never; it ends once the vehicle could accelerate at 0.3 m/s², so a stopped vehicle doesn't creep after a leader that edges forward, and takes a moment to react as a light turns green. Starting and ending at different accelerations, it doesn't flick between stopping and moving off.

  The floor brakes harder than IDM only in its last few centimetres, so a vehicle comes to rest at most about 3 cm short of where IDM would settle; at rest that short of a line, IDM pulls it on at `a·(1 − (s₀/s)²)`, which stays under 0.3 m/s² until it is 14% of its minimum gap short, 7 cm for the shortest gap to lines drawn, 0.5 m. With a floor of 0.5 m/s², as before, it came to rest up to 10 cm short of a line, and with a threshold of 0.2 m/s² moved off from 9% short, so it crept up in a stop or two. Behind a vehicle, with its 3 m minimum gap, it never did. The `stopping` example measures this for each setting.
- **Speed profiles:** each lane has a speed profile, the fastest a driver would go at each point along it: its nominal limit, lowered where it bends to the speed that turns it at 3.5 m/s² sideways, `√(a_lat / κ)`, as AASHTO's side friction factors for intersection curves at 15–25 km/h have it, and no lower than 3 m/s. So limits stay each road's nominal limit, and a bend slows a vehicle wherever it is, on a connector or a road. A lane's curvature is taken at each vertex, its turn over the mean of the segments either side, the profile capped at the lane's limit, and simplified to a few points with straight lines between, within 0.3 m/s of every vertex's speed (Ramer–Douglas–Peucker). Profiles are the longitudinal model's, derived when a simulation is built or restored, not saved, all lanes' points in one list. Drivers accept more sideways on tighter, slower turns than on fast bends (Reymond et al., 2001); one figure is simpler, and easy to change.
- **Lower speeds ahead:** a vehicle's desired speed is the driver's share of the profile at its front. A lower speed ahead on its path, at the driver's share of it, is its own term, `(v² − v_lim²) / 2d` braking for a point of the profile `d` ahead of its front, once that is harsher than comfortable braking, and capped at twice that, so it slows before a lower limit or a bend rather than once on it, braking at about its comfortable deceleration. A profile is lowest at its points, so only they need checking. Points further ahead than it could stop from its speed comfortably can't matter, so the search goes no further. Taking the driver's share of a bend's speed amounts to a share of its sideways acceleration squared, about 0.72 to 1.32 times as much over the usual spread, a plausible spread between drivers.
- **Ghosts:** the search reports every ghost it meets and the caller decides what one means. Checking a gap for a lane change, and in `try_insert`, a ghost is a leader, since a vehicle already indicating into the gap goes first. In the longitudinal model, the nearest ghost before any leader is one the vehicle may make room for.
- **Relaxation:** a vehicle that has just appeared in a lane's traffic, or that another has just appeared in front of, keeps a shorter time headway at first and drops back gradually, as in LMRS (Schakel, Knoop and van Arem, 2012). A record's age starts at zero when the vehicle changes lanes into the lane, enters a merge onto it or joins it from beside it, carries over from one lane to the next, and starts settled for an inserted vehicle. A leader term keeps `h(a)` of the follower's time headway, with `a` the younger of the two records' ages and `h` rising from LMRS's `T_min/T_max`, 0.56/1.2, back to 1 exponentially with a time constant of 25 s. The minimum gap stays whole.
- **Acceleration noise:** the driver's dawdling, added to its free-road term (see [the driver model](driver.md)). Following stays exact, so the noise never brings vehicles closer than IDM would.
- **Friction:** a driver goes at most 30 km/h faster than the traffic ahead in the lane beside it, the average over the next 8 s at that lane's limit, if at least three vehicles are there: the friction effect Jang and Cassidy (2012) measured beside congested lanes.
- **Parameters** come from each vehicle's attributes. `VehicleSpec::passenger_car` gives textbook values: IDM's as calibrated with LMRS, as OpenTrafficSim uses them, a = 1.25 m/s², b = 2.09 m/s², s0 = 3 m, T = 1.2 s.
