# Routing

How vehicles choose their way at forks, and what lane changes their routes need, behind the module boundary `models::routing`. The lane-change model turns those needs into desire (see [Lane changes](lane-change.md)).

Each vehicle carries a `Route`, given at insertion, which chooses the next lane when its path is extended past a lane with several successors; the choice is then part of its path. Route models are separate, in `models::routing`, each replaced by editing it, and say what lane changes a route needs, which the lane-change model turns into desire.

- **Random:** a SplitMix64 generator seeded at insertion, drawn from only at forks, so the same seed takes the same way, and snapshots and replays reproduce it. It chooses only among its own lane's successors that its class may use.
- **Destination:** toward a destination, a set of lanes registered with the simulation (`add_destination`), the route `Route::to` names. Each destination has a route table, worked out when it is registered, outside a step, and again on restore. It has a table for each class, over the lanes the class may use, shared by classes that may use the same lanes, as cars, trucks and buses do but for bus lanes; and a route chooses only among the successors its class may use.
  - **Roads:** each lane says which road it is part of (see [the design](../DESIGN.md), Roads for routing), and lanes of one road are equal for finding a way: each road's time, its lanes' time at their speed limits shared among the lanes that start it, is counted once, on each lane that starts it, and nothing on the lanes after them in it, so every way through a road costs the same, and ways through the same roads tie exactly. This is how SUMO and the old prototype route by roads, then choose among a road's lanes by the lane changes they need. Without it, parallel lanes whose connectors differed by milliseconds made one lane the only way, and drivers in the other changed lanes for nothing. Every lane is part of a road: one that stands alone has a road of its own.
  - **Time to the destination** from the start of every lane, by Dijkstra's algorithm backward from the destination's lanes, along lanes leading in and lanes beside that may change into one. A lane change costs no time, since the vehicle travels on while changing, but a way that needs more changes than there is room for isn't a way: each change needs a car's room to change at the limit, its wheelbase, length and change length (see [the design](../DESIGN.md), Lateral path), before the lanes run out beside each other and before the next change it needs. Between ways equally quick, the one with fewer changes wins. A lane's routing cost (`Lane::cost`) counts as time on it, as a dropped lane's taper's does, so a way round it through a change wins over one through it, where there is room to change, and where there isn't, the way through it is the only one, and drivers take it.
  - **Lane changes needed:** for each lane, how far along it a vehicle's rear axle can be and still reach the destination with one, two, three or four lane changes, beyond its end if they are on lanes after it, four or more counting as four: the old prototype's lane distances, as SUMO's "best lanes" does it. Each lane's come from the lanes after and beside it on its quickest ways, those whose time is its own, done before it in Dijkstra's order: from a lane after it, its distances on; from a lane beside, theirs for one more change, less the room for the change, and no further than the two run beside each other. So a lane that leads elsewhere must be left in time, and a vehicle with no way left from where it is, as one that missed a change in traffic has, simply takes the quickest way from wherever its lanes go, so rerouting needs nothing more. A change that would run on from one stretch of lanes side by side into the next isn't counted: the next counts its own.
  - **Choosing:** at a fork, the quickest successor, then the one needing the fewest lane changes from its start, ties going to the first, so runs repeat; with no way from any, the first, so one with no way left drives on and leaves wherever its lanes take it.
  - **What it needs:** the routing model tells the lane-change model how many changes the vehicle needs from where it is, and within what distance, and the same from level with it on each lane beside it may change to, or that there is no way from there. The route desire weighs them (see [Lane changes](lane-change.md)).
  - **Cost:** five numbers per lane per destination, 20 bytes: about 0.5 ms and 200 KB a destination on the 16 by 16 city of 10,000 lanes.

Random routes need no table, and need no changes: every lane leads somewhere. They pay no heed to routing costs, so on a random route a dropped lane is a zipper.
