# Gap acceptance and priority

When the vehicle at the head of a barrier commits to crossing it, behind the module boundary `models::gap_acceptance`: from the light, gaps in priority traffic, room beyond and the order vehicles arrived. Barriers themselves, commitment, passing, release and coming to rest, are the core's (see [the design](../DESIGN.md), Barriers and signals), and so is the junction's queue, whose contract this model must keep: a vehicle goes ahead only of priority vehicles not yet queued, and behind only those queued.

## When the head commits

- **When it commits,** at each longitudinal update, decided from a read-only view of the state and applied after:

| Barrier          | Commits when                                                                                                                     |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Signal           | The light is green and it can no longer stop at its comfortable deceleration a line gap short of the line; never on amber, nor on red unless it may turn on red (below), when it commits as at a stop line. Until then a green light, with room beyond (below), exerts nothing, and an amber or red one is a stop line. |
| Give-way line    | There is a gap (see Gaps in priority traffic, below)                                                                                 |
| Stop line        | It has come to rest at the line, and there is a gap                                                                              |
| All-way stop     | It has come to rest at the line, no vehicle at the head of another all-way stop line of the junction whose connector conflicts with its own came to rest there earlier, and there is a gap in the traffic that has committed (see Gaps in priority traffic, below) |
| Keep-clear start | It can no longer stop comfortably a line gap short of the zone, and there is room beyond its far end (below). Until then, with room, it exerts nothing |

- **Amber:** a vehicle past the point where it can stop comfortably has committed already, on green, and goes on; the rest stop. As the old prototype had it, nothing commits on amber or red, so a vehicle braking for a line can't creep up to it and then go. That stretch is the dilemma zone ITE's amber time covers at the limit; a driver well over the limit, committing further out, can reach the line just after it turns red, as on the road.
- **Room past a keep-clear zone:** for the vehicle, and every vehicle ahead of it short of the zone's far end, its length and minimum gap each, past the far end. Of the vehicles past it, nearest first: if there is that room behind one now, there is room; if there wouldn't be even once it had gone as far as it would stopping comfortably, there isn't; otherwise only if there is room behind the next one for it too. So a vehicle just past the zone, still moving but about to stop behind a queue, doesn't count as room, while traffic flowing through doesn't slow for it. A vehicle that changes lanes into the zone isn't held by it, as a driver cutting in isn't.
- **Room beyond:** every barrier into a junction is also a keep-clear start, its zone the junction lane: a vehicle doesn't enter unless there is room for it on the lane after, as the road rules require, nor while a vehicle ahead of it on its way through, headed elsewhere, is at rest or will wait inside the junction, as a turner waiting for a gap does, since it couldn't drive through; one that will wait inside itself may draw up behind it.
- **Giving way to vehicles inside:** nor does a vehicle enter while one already in the junction part way through waits to take a lane its barrier gives way to, as drivers give way to vehicles in an intersection: one in the queue only as far as that lane, or committed to its barrier into the junction and not yet in the queue, its next barrier there. The network lists, for each signal barrier, the next stretches of the turners on movements never green with it that cross its way (see [the design](../DESIGN.md), Barriers from control), so once a turner's light is red, the cross street, now green, waits at its line until the turner is queued right through, then follows it. A green light doesn't otherwise ask that a turner be out of the way first. This is driver behaviour, which the junction doesn't rely on. A queue can still back into a junction from beyond it, so **gridlock** stays possible.

**Turning on red,** as in North America and some of Europe: a movement may allow turning on red, so on red its signal barrier is a stop line giving way to every lane its connector's lanes conflict with, the vehicle coming to rest at it and then taking a gap (see Gaps in priority traffic, below), as SUMO's link state for a right turn on red has it. The barrier carries that set beside its own, so the simulation derives nothing. Traffic held at its own red counts for nothing in the gap, as everywhere, and a vehicle at a red light it may turn on isn't one that will surely stop. Released on red only once at rest short of the line, as at a stop line, since a vehicle that has committed to turn moves off slowly enough that it could still stop: so one committed on green and slowed by traffic until the light turns red goes on, as at amber. Compilation allows it on the near-side turns of every signalised intersection where the network says so, which it doesn't by default, whichever side traffic drives on; and on any movement an intersection names, which covers turning left on red from one one-way street into another. The pretty view draws a lane that may turn on red in white while its light is red: open, but giving way.

## Gaps in priority traffic

At a give-way or stop barrier, the head goes only if no vehicle with priority over it could reach its conflicts in time. On its lanes in the junction, up to the next barrier on its path, it gives way to every vehicle on a conflicting lane its barrier gives way to (see [the design](../DESIGN.md), Barriers from control). On any other conflicting lane, and on every lane at an all-way stop, only vehicles that have committed count, queued or moving past every barrier before the conflict, and it goes only behind them, as it can't go ahead of one the junction will order before it.

- **The gap:** it goes if, for each conflict, it can go ahead of or behind every priority vehicle on its way there. Ahead: it can clear the conflict before the priority vehicle could reach it, both accelerating as hard as they can up to their desired speeds. Behind: it could reach the conflict no sooner than the priority vehicle, at its speed now, will have cleared it; one at rest never clears, so nothing goes behind it, and nor does one queued only as far as its waiting point, with the conflict where it will stop there. Taking them nearest first, the first it can go ahead of decides, since those behind arrive later, and one it can neither go ahead of nor behind refuses the gap. Either way the margin is 0.5 s plus the time headway of whichever then follows the other, so the junction, which makes it follow, needn't brake it. Going behind lets a car at an all-way stop move off as the one before it crosses, timed to reach the crossing just as it clears, rather than waiting for it to leave the junction. A vehicle in the junction's queue for the conflict can't be gone ahead of, only behind, on any lane: going, the yielding vehicle joins the queue behind it (see [the design](../DESIGN.md), Barriers inside a junction), so would stop for it in the conflict, creeping past its line only to give way after all. Nor can a vehicle not yet in the queue be gone behind: going, the yielding vehicle would join the queue ahead of it, and the junction would have it give way after all, as a left turner would brake for a right turner that took a gap behind it. So a vehicle goes ahead only of priority vehicles not yet queued, and behind only those queued, and joins the queue where it means to go. It isn't airtight, since a vehicle that commits to its barrier is queued only once it can no longer stop short of its conflict. Vehicles commit late, only once they can no longer stop safely, so one committed is always too near and too fast to be gone ahead of anyway, and one still approaching can stop if the yielding vehicle couldn't clear as it meant to; the rule makes that so by construction rather than by margin. Covering d from speed v at acceleration a up to v_max takes `(√(v² + 2ad) − v) / a`, or, once at v_max, `t₁ + (d − d₁) / v_max` with `t₁ = (v_max − v) / a` and `d₁ = (v + v_max)·t₁ / 2`. It clears a crossing once its rear is past it. From rest, a car turning across a 50 km/h road waits for about 7 s, its time to clear plus 0.5 s plus the oncoming car's 1.2 s headway, close to the 6–7.5 s critical gaps the Highway Capacity Manual gives for turns from a minor road. SUMO judges gaps the same way, from foes' arrival times.
- **Impatience:** the longer a driver waits at rest at its line, the more it reckons that priority vehicles not yet committed will give way to it, as SUMO's `impatience` grows with waiting time: from nothing as it comes to rest to full after `TIME_TO_IMPATIENCE` (30 s), a third as long for a fully assertive driver. Judging whether it can go ahead of one, it reckons it accelerating less, by that fraction, and at full impatience braking at half its comfortable deceleration to stop its minimum gap short, moving or at rest: half, since IDM, following, brakes harder than the constant deceleration that would just stop it. So a driver kept waiting comes to cut in front of traffic, as frustrated drivers do, and the major road's traffic sometimes stops for it, since one not yet committed can always stop. That traffic slows only for a vehicle in the junction's queue, which one creeping off its line joins only once its nose reaches its first conflict, so it can need more than comfortable braking, but no more than safe braking: up to about 3.9 m/s² in the priority scenarios' busy traffic, rarely. That is accepted: letting it slow sooner, for a vehicle committed to its line but not yet queued, would mix the barriers' order into the junction's. It breaks the ties where two drivers each wait on traffic stuck behind the other, as opposite far-side turners at a priority crossroads do, each waiting inside for oncoming traffic held up behind the other, with no red light to end it; the junction keeps whatever follows safe. SUMO waits 180 s by default, which would leave such ties locked for minutes. The arrival record, the step a driver came to rest at its line, gives the waiting time, so a give-way line records it too.
- **Finding priority vehicles:** from the start of each conflict, back along the priority lane's occupants and the lanes leading into it, as the follower search goes (see [the design](../DESIGN.md), Searching back), and only into lanes some path through reaches the conflict. On each branch only the nearest vehicle headed onto the lane matters, since those behind it there arrive later; vehicles whose paths turn off are passed over, as is a body that overlaps the priority lane beside its own, unless one waits in the lane at a barrier it hasn't committed to, such as a turner waiting for its own gap: nobody behind it can come until it goes. Without that, opposite turners would each wait for the queue held up behind the other. A vehicle that a red or amber light holds short of the conflict holds back everyone behind it, so its branch is done: it hasn't committed, and nothing commits on amber or red, so it will surely stop. That keeps a red light's queue from blocking an unprotected turn. A vehicle at rest at a green light, or at a give-way or stop line, may yet go, so it counts. A vehicle the junction holds out of its queue short of the conflict (see [the design](../DESIGN.md), Queue invariants) will surely stop there too. Opposite turners are in neither's set, so don't wait on each other. One whose rear has cleared the conflict is passed over.
- **Unprotected turns,** turning across oncoming traffic on a green with no arrow (filter turns, in Australian usage), are this with the barrier inside the junction: the turner waits at its first conflict with oncoming traffic, at the start of its connector's second lane, and goes through a gap, behind every oncoming vehicle committed meanwhile (see [the design](../DESIGN.md), Barriers inside a junction). Oncoming traffic held at its red light doesn't count, so a protected turn goes freely. A hook turner waits inside the junction too, but at a signal barrier, for the road it turns into.
- **Cost:** only heads at give-way and stop barriers check, at 10 Hz, with one backward search for each conflict with a lane of higher priority.
