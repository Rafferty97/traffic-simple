# Gap acceptance and priority

When the vehicle at the head of a barrier commits to crossing it, behind the module boundary `models::gap_acceptance`: from the light, gaps in priority traffic, room beyond and the order vehicles arrived. Barriers themselves, commitment, passing, release and coming to rest, are the core's (see [the design](../DESIGN.md), Barriers and signals), and so is the junction's queue, whose contract this model must keep: a vehicle goes ahead only of priority vehicles not yet queued, and behind only those queued.

## When the head commits

At each longitudinal update, for the nearest barrier on its path the head hasn't committed to, the model answers one of four ways: **commit**, and the barrier isn't there for it from then on; **pass** for now, held by nothing though not committed, as at a green light it can still stop for or a keep-clear zone with room past it, and only those; **yield** to a vehicle, the first with priority it found no gap before or behind, or one in the junction it gives way to; or **stop**, for no vehicle in particular, as at a red or amber light or with no room beyond. The core holds a vehicle that yields or stops short of the line.

- **When it commits,** at each longitudinal update, decided from a read-only view of the state and applied after:

| Barrier          | Commits when                                                                                                                     |
| ---------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Signal           | The light is green and it can no longer stop at its comfortable deceleration a line gap short of the line; never on amber, nor on red unless it may turn on red (below), when it commits as at a stop line. Until then a green light, with room beyond (below), exerts nothing, and an amber or red one is a stop line. |
| Give-way line    | There is a gap, in the traffic with priority and in the traffic of equal priority that came to rest first (see Gaps in priority traffic, below) |
| Stop line        | It has come to rest at the line, and there is a gap; an all-way stop is stop lines with nothing of higher priority |
| Keep-clear start | It can no longer stop comfortably a line gap short of the zone, and there is room beyond its far end (below). Until then, with room, it exerts nothing |

- **Amber:** a vehicle past the point where it can stop comfortably has committed already, on green, and goes on; the rest stop. As the old prototype had it, nothing commits on amber or red, so a vehicle braking for a line can't creep up to it and then go. That stretch is the dilemma zone ITE's amber time covers at the limit; a driver well over the limit, committing further out, can reach the line just after it turns red, as on the road.
- **Room past a keep-clear zone:** for the vehicle, and every vehicle ahead of it short of the zone's far end, its length and minimum gap each, past the far end. Of the vehicles past it, nearest first: if there is that room behind one now, there is room; if there wouldn't be even once it had gone as far as it would stopping comfortably, there isn't; otherwise only if there is room behind the next one for it too. So a vehicle just past the zone, still moving but about to stop behind a queue, doesn't count as room, while traffic flowing through doesn't slow for it. A vehicle that changes lanes into the zone isn't held by it, as a driver cutting in isn't.
- **Room beyond:** every barrier into a junction is also a keep-clear start, its zone the junction lane: a vehicle doesn't enter unless there is room for it on the lane after, as the road rules require, along its path from the lane's start as far as it knows it, among the vehicles there, nearest first: there is room behind one if there is now, none if there wouldn't be even once it had gone on as far as braking comfortably would take it, and otherwise room only if there is room behind the next one for it too, so a vehicle rolling up behind a stopped one isn't counted on to move on further than it can; the room is for itself, the vehicles ahead of it on its own way through, and those from other ways in bound for the same lane that will go before it: above it in the junction's queue, or, unless it is queued itself, committed to their way in, either way with nothing left before that lane that could hold them. One that may yet wait, as a turner waiting inside for a gap does, isn't counted: it might be waiting for this vehicle's own traffic, and each would wait on the other. So of two ways in each seeing room for one, the one coming second waits at its line, as drivers see the room about to be taken by traffic already moving to fill it, and as SUMO keeps vehicles out of a junction whose exit its approaching foes will fill. Nor does it enter while a vehicle ahead of it on its way through, headed elsewhere, is at rest or will wait inside the junction, as a turner waiting for a gap does, since it couldn't drive through; one that will wait inside itself may draw up behind it. A body counts only on a lane it is headed along or leaving, as the junction has it: one overlapping a branch beside its own, as at a fork, isn't in the way. It is checked only at a line: an uncontrolled intersection has none, nor does a priority road's way through a priority intersection, so their traffic drives on in, as drivers do, and can stop in the junction behind a queue. Where traffic must keep clear, the network says so with a keep-clear zone.
- **Giving way to vehicles inside:** nor does a vehicle enter while one already in the junction part way through waits to take a lane its barrier gives way to, as drivers give way to vehicles in an intersection: one inside the junction whose next barrier it hasn't committed to is on that lane, or committed to its barrier into the junction and not yet in the queue, its next barrier there. The network lists, for each signal barrier, the connectors of the turners on movements never green with it whose way past their waiting lines crosses its own (see [the design](../DESIGN.md), Barriers from control), so once a turner's light is red, the cross street, now green, waits at its line until the turner has taken its gap, then follows it. A green light doesn't otherwise ask that a turner be out of the way first. This is driver behaviour, which the junction doesn't rely on. A queue can still back into a junction from beyond it, so **gridlock** stays possible.

**Turning on red,** as in North America and some of Europe: a movement may allow turning on red, so on red its signal barrier is a stop line giving way to every lane its connector's lanes conflict with, the vehicle coming to rest at it and then taking a gap (see Gaps in priority traffic, below), as SUMO's link state for a right turn on red has it. The barrier carries that set beside its own, so the simulation derives nothing. Traffic held at its own red counts for nothing in the gap, as everywhere, and a vehicle at a red light it may turn on isn't one that will surely stop. Released on red only once at rest short of the line, as at a stop line, since a vehicle that has committed to turn moves off slowly enough that it could still stop: so one committed on green and slowed by traffic until the light turns red goes on, as at amber. Compilation allows it on the near-side turns of every signalised intersection where the network says so, which it doesn't by default, whichever side traffic drives on; and on any movement an intersection names, which covers turning left on red from one one-way street into another. The pretty view draws a lane that may turn on red in white while its light is red: open, but giving way.

## Gaps in priority traffic

At a give-way or stop barrier, the head goes only if no vehicle with priority over it could reach its conflicts in time. On its lanes in the junction, up to the next barrier on its path, it gives way to every vehicle on a conflicting lane its barrier gives way to (see [the design](../DESIGN.md), Barriers from control). On a conflicting lane of equal priority, one its barrier takes turns with, it does the same, except that a vehicle that hasn't committed to its own line gives way to it if it came to rest at its line first, and impatience counts for nothing there, since taking turns can't lock up. On any other conflicting lane only vehicles that have committed count, those that can no longer give way to it or moving past every barrier before the conflict, and it goes only behind them, as it can't go ahead of one the junction will order before it.

- **Taking turns:** vehicles of equal priority go in the order they came to rest at their lines; in the same step, by vehicle, so runs repeat. One at rest goes before one still on its way, which gives way to it; two on their way that would meet at their conflict each count the other as priority traffic, so both stop, and the first to come to rest goes. The gap check's own timing keeps one far off, or slow, from holding a vehicle up. Road rules settle conflicts between equal approaches by turn rules, which the priority levels already encode, then often by giving way to the right, and SUMO has right-before-left junctions; taking turns by arrival is simpler and symmetric, and it is the rule at all-way stops, which here are its special case. Drivers that would roll through and settle a meeting by eye both stop instead, which costs a little throughput where it happens.

- **The gap:** it goes if, for each conflict, it can go ahead of or behind every priority vehicle on its way there. Ahead: it can clear the conflict before the priority vehicle could reach it, both accelerating as hard as they can up to their desired speeds. Behind: it could reach the conflict no sooner than the priority vehicle, at its speed now, will have cleared it; one at rest never clears, so nothing goes behind it. Taking them nearest first, the first it can go ahead of decides, since those behind arrive later, and one it can neither go ahead of nor behind refuses the gap. Either way the margin is 0.5 s plus the time headway of whichever then follows the other, so the junction, which makes it follow, needn't brake it. Going behind lets a car at an all-way stop move off as the one before it crosses, timed to reach the crossing just as it clears, rather than waiting for it to leave the junction. A vehicle that can no longer give way to the yielding one can't be gone ahead of, only behind, on any lane: it is in the junction's queue and their pair decided its way, or would be once the yielding vehicle joined the queue below it (see [the design](../DESIGN.md), Conflicts, merging and junctions), so going ahead would only mean stopping for it in the conflict after all. One the junction's order puts after the yielding vehicle, through any chain of decided pairs and vehicles following one another, is passed over, and so is everyone behind it on that way in, since none of them can reach the conflict first. Any other is judged by arrival times, and the junction settles what the judgement misses: once either can no longer give way comfortably, the one needing clearly more braking to give way goes first. Covering d from speed v at acceleration a up to v_max takes `(√(v² + 2ad) − v) / a`, or, once at v_max, `t₁ + (d − d₁) / v_max` with `t₁ = (v_max − v) / a` and `d₁ = (v + v_max)·t₁ / 2`. It clears a crossing once its rear is past it. From rest, a car turning across a 50 km/h road waits for about 7 s, its time to clear plus 0.5 s plus the oncoming car's 1.2 s headway, close to the 6–7.5 s critical gaps the Highway Capacity Manual gives for turns from a minor road. SUMO judges gaps the same way, from foes' arrival times.
- **Impatience:** the longer a driver waits at rest at its line, the more it reckons that priority vehicles not yet committed will give way to it, as SUMO's `impatience` grows with waiting time: from nothing as it comes to rest to full after `TIME_TO_IMPATIENCE` (30 s), a third as long for a fully assertive driver. Judging whether it can go ahead of one, it reckons it accelerating less, by that fraction, and at full impatience braking at half its comfortable deceleration to stop its minimum gap short, moving or at rest: half, since IDM, following, brakes harder than the constant deceleration that would just stop it. So a driver kept waiting comes to cut in front of traffic, as frustrated drivers do, and the major road's traffic sometimes stops for it, since one not yet committed can always stop. That traffic slows for it only once the junction decides their pair, so it can need more than comfortable braking; up to safe braking is accepted. It breaks the ties where two drivers each wait on traffic stuck behind the other, as opposite far-side turners at a priority crossroads do, each waiting inside for oncoming traffic held up behind the other, with no red light to end it; the junction keeps whatever follows safe. SUMO waits 180 s by default, which would leave such ties locked for minutes. The arrival record, the step a driver came to rest at its line, gives the waiting time, so a give-way line records it too.
- **Finding priority vehicles:** from the start of each conflict, back along the priority lane's occupants and the lanes leading into it, as the follower search goes (see [the design](../DESIGN.md), Searching back), and only into lanes some path through reaches the conflict. On each branch only the nearest vehicle headed onto the lane matters, since those behind it there arrive later; vehicles whose paths turn off are passed over, as is a body that overlaps the priority lane beside its own, unless one waits in the lane at a barrier it hasn't committed to, such as a turner waiting for its own gap: nobody behind it can come until it goes. Without that, opposite turners would each wait for the queue held up behind the other. A vehicle that a red or amber light holds short of the conflict holds back everyone behind it, so its branch is done: it hasn't committed, and nothing commits on amber or red, so it will surely stop. That keeps a red light's queue from blocking an unprotected turn. A vehicle at rest at a green light, or at a give-way or stop line, may yet go, so it counts. Once the search meets a vehicle the junction's order puts after the yielding one, its branch is done too: everyone behind it gives way as well. Opposite turners are in neither's set, so don't wait on each other. One whose rear has cleared the conflict is passed over.
- **Unprotected turns,** turning across oncoming traffic on a green with no arrow (filter turns, in Australian usage), are this with the barrier inside the junction: the turner waits at its first conflict with oncoming traffic, partway along its connector, and goes through a gap, behind every oncoming vehicle that can no longer give way to it (see [the design](../DESIGN.md), Barriers inside a junction). Oncoming traffic held at its red light doesn't count, so a protected turn goes freely. A hook turner waits inside the junction too, but at a signal barrier, for the road it turns into.
- **Cost:** only heads at give-way and stop barriers check, at 10 Hz, with one backward search for each conflict with a lane of higher priority.
