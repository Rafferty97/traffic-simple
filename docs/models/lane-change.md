# Lane-change model: LMRS

Whether and when drivers change lanes, behind the module boundary `models::lane_change`. The core mechanics it relies on, where changes may happen, the jump onto the new lane, the lateral path and the old side, are in [the design](../DESIGN.md), Lane membership, lane changes and diverging, and how vehicles keep apart while they change is in [Multi-lane car following](../multilane-car-following.md).

Drivers decide as in LMRS, the Lane-change Model with Relaxation and Synchronization (Schakel, Knoop and van Arem, 2012), with its published parameters. At each update every vehicle weighs up both sides cheaply, and only one whose desire has reached its threshold checks the gap, which takes searches.

1. **Desire** to change to each side, from −1 to 1, is the sum of incentives, each in units of desire:
   - **Route,** `d_r`: toward the neighbour from which its route needs fewer lane changes, by its route table (see [Routing](routing.md)). For each change still needed, it rises linearly from 0 to 1 toward the point by which that change must be done, by distance (295 m per change to make) or time at its speed (43 s), whichever is higher, and the most urgent counts. A lane that ends by merging and is left early counts as ending there, so vehicles move out of it along the way. **Built so far:** only the lane left early, toward the neighbour that goes on.
   - **Speed,** `d_s`: how much faster traffic goes ahead in the lane beside it than in its own, over 69.6 km/h. Each lane's speed is the average of its vehicles over the next 8 s at its limit, no faster than the driver would like, and as fast as it would like where nobody is: an average, not the leader alone, so a driver moves over before its own lane has stopped. Each lane's speeds are summed in order once per update, so an average is two binary searches.
   - **Keeping to a side,** `d_b`: the free threshold toward the side the lane says to keep to.
   - **Courtesy:** letting by the vehicle it holds up most, its old follower once it changes, by what that follower would gain, weighed by its politeness, in units of its comfortable deceleration.

   As LMRS has it, `d = d_r + θ·(d_s + d_b + d_c)`: the voluntary incentives count fully unless they work against the route, and not at all once the route's desire is strong enough to cooperate. The desire follows the incentives smoothed over 1 s, so a fleeting gain doesn't make a driver change.
2. **Thresholds:** at `d_free`, 0.365, the driver checks the gap and, if it lets it, indicates, and changes after indicating for at least 0.5 s. At `d_sync`, 0.577, it indicates even without a gap, asking for room, and synchronises: it adjusts its speed to the leader it would have in the target lane, its headway shortened by its desire, no harder than comfortably. At `d_coop`, 0.788, followers in the target lane make room for it, and it changes even without room to finish. It cancels once its desire falls below half its threshold, or no permitted neighbour remains on that side: none it may change into, or none it could finish a change into before the lanes beside its path run out or reach a junction. So a vehicle committed to a junction doesn't hold itself back to line up with the lane beside, as a driver who has missed the chance stops trying.
3. **Gap acceptance:** a driver whose desire will reach its threshold by its next update, as its smoothing has it, and that won't be cooling down after a refused check, asks through its intent for the gap that way to be measured, and the core measures it at the start of that update, just before the model judges it; indicating, it asks once it may check again, and has its leader in the target lane measured at every update, for synchronising. So its gap is measured, on what it sees then, by the time it first checks: asking only at the threshold, and checking an update later, made more drivers from either side change into the same spot and brake hard for each other on the busy ring. The gap is measured as if the driver were already settled in the target lane, level with where it is: its own acceleration there, against its leaders and any ghost ahead, and its new follower's with it as a leader, found by searching back. Both keep their time headways shortened by its desire, from the whole at no desire to `T_min/T_max` at a desire of 1, and the change is accepted if neither brakes harder than the desire times the driver's comfortable deceleration, raised toward b_safe (4 m/s²) by its assertiveness.
4. **Spreading the checks:** a refused check waits a few updates before the next, 0.2 s scaled by `1 + politeness − assertiveness/2`, so drivers' checks don't fall in step. A vehicle with no lane it may change to and no desire left is skipped outright.

- **Room to finish:** a vehicle doesn't change lanes unless its rear axle would be past the quintic's midpoint before it had to stop behind its leader: the gap less its minimum gap, plus the leader's stopping distance at its comfortable deceleration. Otherwise it would be left straddling both lanes, blocking them. It may indicate without the room. While it wants to change, indicating or with half its threshold of desire, which its intent says, the longitudinal model keeps it room behind its leaders to get halfway through a change from rest, so it isn't boxed in: a separate term, with that room as its minimum gap, which IDM settles on from above, never braking harder than comfortably, since it is a preference, not safety, which only binds in slow traffic. VISSIM has a minimum front clearance for changing lanes from a standstill, and drivers are taught to stop where they can see the tyres of the vehicle ahead.
- **Assertiveness:** drivers vary in how readily they change lanes, from 0 to 1, as SUMO's `lcAssertive` and `lcSpeedGain`: an assertive driver's thresholds are lower, by up to half, it accepts harder braking, and it waits less after a refusal. A few assertive drivers pull out into a faster lane and slow it, so a fast lane doesn't form, and the cautious follow. Its distribution is the caller's concern; the UI draws it as a uniform number cubed, about one driver in ten above 0.7.
- **Making room,** LMRS's cooperation: a follower whose search reaches a ghost before any leader may make room for it, treating it as a leader, if its owner's desire has reached `d_coop`. It starts if that takes no more braking than its courtesy, a per-driver attribute (0 never yields), and once it has chosen to, it keeps making room up to b_safe until the ghost is gone. Typically a few nearer followers drive on and a later one opens a gap. This is Hidas's (2002, 2005) cooperative lane changing, and SUMO's `lcCooperative`.
- **Two vehicles into one gap:** a ghost ahead counts as a leader in a vehicle's own gap check, but the follower search passes ghosts over, so of two indicating into the same gap, the one behind waits.

**Module boundary:** the whole of LMRS lives in the lane-change model; the rest of the simulation sees only each vehicle's intent, whether it wants to change or is indicating, and to which side, and the model's own state, opaque to it. The model searches nothing itself. At each lane-change update the core first measures what a change would do for each driver whose intent asks (a `Prospect`: its own acceleration in the target lane, its new follower's, the room to finish and its leader there); then the model measures each driver's incentives cheaply, moves the desire (`aim`), decides whether to check a gap (`step`), judges the measured gap (`resolve`) and says what to do: nothing, indicate, cancel or change, which side it wants and whether it wants a gap measured next time, which the simulation carries out and records in the intent. At each longitudinal update it adds its terms, synchronisation, toward the leader measured in the target lane, and making room, toward a ghost the core's search found ahead, to each driver's acceleration, as junctions and merges add theirs. This is SUMO's split between its lane changer and its lane-change models.
